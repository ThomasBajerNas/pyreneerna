Château de Quéribus

Terrifying

From the 2014 guide – ThomasTerrifying.

The last Cathar castle to fall, in 1255. It sits on a rocky pinnacle at 728 metres with views to the Mediterranean and the Canigou on a clear day. Windy – bring a jumper.

The history of the Cathars

Quéribus was the last castle to hold out, until 1255 – eleven years after Montségur. It was then rebuilt by the king to guard the border.

UNESCO World Heritage

Royal Capetian Fortresses of Languedoc

World Heritage since
2026

Eight fortresses that the French kings built or rebuilt in the 13th and 14th centuries after the Albigensian Crusade, to hold the border with the Kingdom of Aragon: the castle in Carcassonne and seven sentinels on rocky ridges – Aguilar, Lastours, Montségur, Peyrepertuse, Puilaurens, Quéribus and Termes. They are often called Cathar castles, but the walls you see today are mostly the king's, built after the fall of the Cathars. World Heritage since July 2026.

Visit: All the castles charge an entrance fee and sit on steep summits – allow a 15–45 minute walk uphill in sun and wind. Peyrepertuse and Quéribus may close when the tramontane is blowing hard.
