Canal du Midi

World Heritage from Toulouse to the Mediterranean

Pierre-Paul Riquet from Béziers built the canal in 1666–1681. It's 240 kilometres long and links Toulouse with the Étang de Thau. Hire a boat without a licence in, for example, Homps or Capestang, or cycle the towpath. World Heritage since 1996.

UNESCO World Heritage

Canal du Midi

World Heritage since
1996

The 240-kilometre canal from Toulouse to the Étang de Thau was dug in 1666–1681 under the direction of Pierre-Paul Riquet and was one of the greatest engineering feats of its time: 63 locks, aqueducts, a tunnel and dams in the Montagne Noire that supply the canal with water. Together with the Canal de Garonne it links the Atlantic and the Mediterranean.

Visit: The towpath along the canal is perfect for cycling – long stretches are tarmac or compacted gravel. You can hire a canal boat without a boat licence. Don't miss the Fonserannes lock staircase near Béziers.
