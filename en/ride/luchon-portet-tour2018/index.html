The Tour's 65-kilometre stage: Luchon–Col de Portet

65 km · 3,000 m · Bagnères-de-Luchon

The short but brutal stage 17 of the 2018 Tour de France: only 65 km but about 3000 metres of climbing. First the Peyresourde from Luchon, then the steep Val Louron-Azet (7.4 km at over 8 per cent) and finally the Col de Portet above Saint-Lary, 16 km at almost 9 per cent to 2215 metres. PJAMM ranks the Portet as the second-hardest cycling climb in France, and it offers fully alpine surroundings with lakes and wide views. It's used as a summit finish in the Tour (2018 and 2021).

TipsStock up in Loudenvielle and Saint-Lary; no services on the Portet. The last kilometres are above 2000 metres and can be cold and windy even in July. The route is one-way: arrange a pick-up or roll back via Arreau and the Peyresourde (another 50 km or so and 900 hm). The Portet road often closes early in the autumn. Shorter alternative: Pla d'Adet (1680 m) from Saint-Lary, which shares the start of the Portet road.
